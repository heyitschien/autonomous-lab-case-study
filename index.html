01 · Executive scan

The signal in sixty seconds.

This is not a story about asking a model to build a trading bot. It is a case study in implementation leadership: turning uncertainty into a system that knows its boundaries, exposes its evidence, and stops when proof is missing.

What the work demonstrates

  • Requirements and architecture under real ambiguity
  • AI-agent coordination with bounded roles and traceable handoffs
  • Expected-versus-actual validation and evidence review
  • Risk-aware release discipline and human accountability

What it does not claim

02 · The business problem

Make a moving system legible, then make it governable.

The domain is market research. The transferable challenge is broader: many AI implementations fail because the data, decision rights, and definition of proof remain vague.

In plain English: before asking AI to reason, I made sure the system could tell what happened, where the facts came from, what it was allowed to do, and who could approve the next step.
01

The first product helped a human orient

A decision-support “Coach” combined structured market facts with on-demand model explanation. It proved the usefulness of deterministic facts first—and exposed the ceiling created by weak provenance and freshness.

02

The lab separated research from authority

The autonomous lab became a distinct system with a harder rule: models can contribute intelligence, but irreversible decisions remain outside the model layer.

03

The roadmap became a sequence of gates

Instead of building a monolith, the work moved through data contracts, deterministic state, auditability, risk rules, strategy, planning, simulation, evaluation, and readiness review.

04

Every failure had to improve the institution

A defect was not closed with a patch alone. It produced a regression test, stronger acceptance criteria, a clearer owner, or a safer stop condition.

03 · Architecture

A deterministic spine. Models beside it.

The architecture is intentionally asymmetrical. Code owns facts, state, risk, audit, and future execution planning. Models operate in a shadow research plane where they can classify, explain, challenge, and propose without gaining runtime authority.

A model can disagree with the deterministic system. It cannot override it. Any useful proposal must survive a contract, evidence, review, and human promotion path.

04 · Implementation method

The work moves in inspectable slices.

The core capability is not code generation. It is the operating layer around AI: deciding what matters, translating it into contracts, routing the right work, judging the evidence, and protecting the final decision.

01Frame

Clarify the user, objective, constraints, and consequence.

02Contract

Define behavior, boundaries, owner, and acceptance evidence.

03Build

Implement the smallest useful, reviewable slice.

04Observe

Compare expected and actual behavior with durable evidence.

05Challenge

Use independent review against the exact commit.

06Correct

Turn gaps into tests, stronger judges, or explicit stops.

07Promote

A human decides whether the evidence earns the next state.

05 · Failure as evidence

The strongest proof is what changed after something went wrong.

A polished case study should not hide the seams. These incidents show the system learning: each one sharpened a test, validator, contract, or review rule.

INCIDENT 01

An API value meant something different than assumed.

The integration compiled, but the external date value was interpreted with the wrong semantics. The system rejected all telemetry rather than accepting corrupt input.

Changed
72-assertion regression coverage and a rule to verify external semantics against docs, fixtures, and live probes.
Signal
Availability is not understanding.
INCIDENT 02

A capture policy accepted a weak proxy.

Samples appeared to advance while the replay clock had not moved. The validator caught the mismatch and exposed a loose evidence-capture rule.

Changed
Strictly later chart time became required before a sample could satisfy the gate.
Signal
A passing check must prove the exact claim.
INCIDENT 03

The evidence judge needed its own adversarial tests.

Existence checks and timing assumptions could certify artifacts that did not actually establish the promised behavior.

Changed
Assertions became bound to precise artifacts, chronology, hashes, and a paused-first invariant.
Signal
Test the tester.
INCIDENT 04

“Executed” was mistaken for “passed.”

A validation command could run, exit non-zero, and still appear complete. Independent review blocked the work before promotion.

Changed
Completion now requires the result—not merely the presence—of validation evidence.
Signal
Review is part of the product.
